Practice Trading Guide

This shows you how to place practice trades yourself on the Trade Deck. No real money moves, and nothing is sent to a broker.

Read this first

  • It is practice only. Prices come from real option quotes, but the trades are pretend. Real options trading is for adults.
  • Options are risky. You can lose everything you paid for a trade, sometimes within minutes.
  • Nothing here predicts the market. In testing, the lit-up signals did not make enough to cover trading costs. The support and resistance lines have not been tested at all.
  • The goal is to learn, and to find out how your choices really turn out.

Words you need

WordWhat it means
StockA small piece of a company. SPY and QQQ are funds that hold many stocks.
OptionA contract that changes in value as a stock moves. It has an end date.
CALLYou are betting the stock will go up.
PUTYou are betting the stock will go down.
StrikeThe stock price written into the contract. The site picks one near the current price.
ExpiryThe day the contract ends. For SPY and QQQ it is usually the same day.
ContractOne option. It covers 100 shares, so a price of $1.20 costs $120.
AskThe price you pay to buy.
BidThe price you get when you sell. It is lower than the ask.

Because you buy at the ask and sell at the bid, a new trade usually starts out a little negative. That gap is a real cost.

An option can also lose value when the stock barely moves. As the end of the day gets closer, there is less time left, and the price tends to shrink. Being right about direction does not guarantee a gain.

Your first practice trade

  1. Open your private link. Keep it to yourself, because anyone who has it can trade on your page.
  2. Check that the market is open.
  3. Look at the Guardrails and leave them on.
  4. In Signals, set Contracts to 1 with the − and + buttons.
  5. Pick a stock. Decide: do you think it will go up (CALL) or down (PUT)?
  6. Before you buy, say your reason out loud, and how much you are willing to lose.
  7. Tap CALL or PUT. The button changes to Buy 1?
  8. Tap it again quickly. A message shows what you bought and the price.

You do not pick the strike or the expiry. The site chooses a contract near the stock price that fits the limits.

Limits (the standard settings): 1 to 3 contracts per order, up to 3 trades open at once, and $1,200 per order. One contract can cost up to $400 for SPY and QQQ, or $800 for other stocks. If an order is refused, the message tells you why.

Reading your trade card

  • The big number is your gain or loss in dollars, with the percent beside it.
  • The bar shows where you are between your stop (left), breakeven (middle) and your gain line (right).
  • Paid is what you bought at. Now (sell price) is what you would get now. Best so far is the highest sell price since you bought.
  • Prices update about every 20 seconds, so they can lag behind the market.

Selling

  • With one contract: tap Close position, then confirm.
  • With two or three: tap how many to sell (for example 1, or All 3), then confirm. Selling part of a trade lets you keep some money from it while the rest stays open.
  • Anything still open is sold automatically at 3:59 PM New York time on a normal day. Some holiday-week days close early at 1:00 PM. The site does not adjust for that, so sell before 1:00 PM yourself on those days.

Guardrails

SettingStandardWhat it does
Safety stop30%Sells if the price drops 30% below what you paid.
Trail the gainstarts at +100%At +100% it does not sell yet. It starts following your best price, and sells if the price falls 25% from that best.
Close all at gain+100%The other choice: sells everything as soon as you reach the gain line.

You can change the percentages. A change applies to all your open trades right away.

Example (made-up numbers). You buy 1 CALL at $1.20, so it costs $120.

  • The sell price might be $1.16 right away, so the card shows −$4.
  • The 30% stop is at $0.84. Selling there loses $36.
  • The +100% line is at $2.40. With Trail the gain, suppose it climbs to $3.00. The trailing stop is then $2.25, and selling there gains $105.
  • If it turns back right after $2.40, the trailing stop is $1.80, and selling there gains $60.

Stops are not a promise. The site checks about every 20 seconds, so a fast drop can sell you well below your stop. Stops only work while the server is running. If guardrails are OFF, nothing sells for you until 3:59 PM.

Signals

Signals describe what the stock has been doing. They are not instructions, and they cannot see the future.

Each row has four small squares. Green means that clue points up, red means down, and grey means flat.

SquareClueIn plain words
VVWAPCompares the price with the day's average price, giving more weight to busy moments.
OOpening rangeCompares the price with the high and low of the first 30 minutes.
EEMA 9/21Compares a faster moving average with a slower one.
MMACDAnother way to measure whether momentum is building or fading.

When at least 3 of the 4 agree, that sets the trend. The arrow shows it: green up, red down, or a slash for mixed.

  • H½ (SPY and QQQ only): which way the first half hour went, compared with the day before.
  • Flag: the stock looks stretched. It may have moved far, fast. This uses two tools called RSI and Bollinger Bands.
WordWhat it means
BUYThe trend is up and not stretched upward.
SELLThe trend is down and not stretched downward. It does not mean “sell your trade.”
LATEThere is a trend, but the price is already stretched in that direction.
WATCHThe clues are split, and the price is stretched.
A dashNothing lines up.
staleThere is no fresh data. Do not use that row.

Support and resistance

  • Support is a price below the current one where some traders expect a falling stock might slow down.
  • Resistance is a price above it where a rising stock might slow down.

Prices often go straight through these lines. Think of them as landmarks, not walls.

On each trade card there is a flat line:

  • The dot is the stock price now.
  • Green marks are below the price. Red marks are above it.
  • Taller marks are kinds of levels that more people tend to watch. Height does not prove a level is stronger.
  • Hover or tap a mark to see its name and price. All levels opens the full list.
  • These are stock prices, not the price of your option.
NameWhat it is

The label looks up for a CALL (resistance) and down for a PUT (support):

  • Near or Approaching: the price is close to a major level. Pay attention. It might pause, bounce or break through.
  • Room above or Room below: the next major level in that direction is farther away. It does not mean the price will get there.

A simple practice routine

  1. Wait and watch the first 30 minutes (until 10:00 AM New York time). The opening range is not ready before then.
  2. Look at the trend arrow and the status word.
  3. Check for a flag, which means the move may be stretched.
  4. Plan before buying: why you are buying, how much you are willing to lose, and where you will take a gain.
  5. Buy 1 contract.
  6. Find the levels. They show on your trade card, so you see them after you buy. Note the nearest major level above and below, and watch what the price does there.
  7. Follow your plan. Do not move your exit just because you are hoping.
  8. Write it down: the time, stock, CALL or PUT, your reason, the result, and whether you followed the plan.
  9. Review after at least 20 trades. Add up all of them, including the bad days. Even 20 trades can be mostly luck.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Buying again right away to win back a loss.
  • Turning guardrails off because you hope a loser will come back.
  • Buying 3 contracts before you understand 1.
  • Buying only because a row lit up.
  • Chasing the daily goal. A day with no trades is fine.

If you feel upset or rushed, tap Stop for the day.
